The problems

parser: the shared parser reads month first

Every tenant on file writes the day first. The shared parser reads every date month first, so a date whose day is 12 or lower comes out with day and month swapped: “Our 7 April reminder went out on 4 July” (R-41200). This is the fix #4471 already has queued. It covers reports from the app and email channels for every tenant, and from the form for tenants on booking-v2.

hint: the booking-v3 form tells day-first tenants to type month first

Tenants on booking-v3 show MM/DD/YYYY under the date field. Their people type what the hint says, and the form reads it the tenant’s way, day first. The result looks the same as parser (a swapped day and month), but the parser is not involved: the fix is the hint (and the field’s input order) following the tenant’s locale. Only reports through the form at booking-v3 tenants land here. CauseTable already shows this group as “not this ticket — no ticket of its own”.

one-day: the date moves by one day

These reports have no swap: “Invite says Tuesday 12 May. Calendar says Monday 11 May” (R-41202), “Client in Lisbon got Tuesday. Client in Toronto got Monday. Same meeting” (R-41344), “Every booking in June is one day off” (R-41209). A day that moves by one looks like a time-zone or daylight-saving problem in how invites are sent, not a parsing one. The desk had read these apart (oneDayMove), but the #4471 table still counts them under parser, so a parser fix would have been expected to close them.

Intake tags are not the cause

Intake tags each report from the reporter’s browser. The tenant decides the cause, not the browser: the notes under the table count how many reports intake tagged as the other cause. Do not triage these problems by tag.
